01 · BACKGROUND — THE WORK

The month-end close is verification work

Every month, controllers reconcile bank accounts, book accrual and amortization entries, explain budget variances, and roll prepaid schedules forward. The work is evidence-bound: every number must tie to a statement line, a bill, a payroll register.

It is exactly the work firms want agents to absorb — high-volume, deadline-driven, procedural. And it is work where an agent that is plausible but wrong is worse than no agent at all.

02 · BACKGROUND — WHAT'S KNOWN

An agent's playbook is just a document —
so it can rewrite itself

APEX-ACCOUNTING · 2026

Dropped agents into a synthetic firm's close. Swapping in a production-grade harness moved scores by ~1 point — those tasks are reasoning-bound; orchestration barely mattered.

RECURSIVE HARNESS IMPROVEMENT · 2026

A harness is just a text document — roles, tool rosters, contracts. A frontier model can rewrite it from feedback, so the scaffold itself becomes the thing that learns.

Put together: on tasks where orchestration is the bottleneck, a harness should be able to improve itself. But improve against what signal?

03 · THE PROBLEM

In production there is no gold at training time

  • Nobody grades your close. The "right answer" to a reconciliation is not known until an auditor — or a restatement — finds otherwise.
  • So self-improvement must run on a stand-in score: an AI grader reviewing the team's work and scoring it — without ever seeing the right answer.
  • Danger: the loop then optimizes what the judge can see — polish, internal consistency, verification-shaped prose — not what is true.

Research question: how far can harness self-improvement go on a gold-blind reward — and what limits it when it stalls? To answer it you need ground truth the loop can never touch — which real books don't offer. So we built a world where it exists.

05 · METHOD — THE SYSTEM

Five parts, one loop

1 · The PlaybookA plain text document describing a small AI team: who reads the files, who does the math, who double-checks — and what each hands the next.
2 · The TeamRuns the playbook to close the books of three brand-new companies each round — reading files, computing, reconciling.
3 · The GraderScores the team's work using only the same files the team saw. It never sees the answer key — how it grades turns out to be the crux of this project.
4 · The CoachReads the grader's notes and makes small, targeted edits to the playbook. It can also revert to any earlier version.
5 · The TryoutNew playbook vs current playbook on identical fresh books. Winner becomes the playbook for the next round.
🔒 The sealed answer key  Because our worlds are generated, the true answers exist — locked away. They are used ONLY to measure progress on 6 never-seen companies. The team, grader, and coach never see them. That's how we know real improvement from convincing-looking improvement.

06 · METHOD — THE REWARD

A grader that only reads can't tell
correct from convincing

The only signal the coach ever gets is the grader's score on each round's training tasks — the reward. Everything hinges on whether that reward tracks the truth.

We ran the loop twice: once with a grader that just reads the work (Run A), once with a grader that must redo the math before scoring (Run B). The verifying grader is harsher — and honest.

Grader score /10 per training round. A's reward drifts in a narrow band regardless of true quality — its correlation with the sealed answer key is only +0.24. B's grader starts harsher (4.9) because it catches real errors; its correlation with truth is +0.70.

08 · INSIGHT → SOLUTION EXPERIMENT B · LIVE

Self-improvement is bounded by how well
its grader can verify — so we upgraded the grader

The limit was never the coach's intelligence or the team's size. It was whether the reward could tell correct from convincing. So we changed one thing: the grader must redo the math — recompute the statement balance, re-add the ledger, confirm every claimed item exists — before it may score.

correlation between the grader's score and the sealed answer key, on identical work — a ~3× jump, at ~2¢ per grading
